## 1. How does FanDuel list and settle "To Record a Double-Double," and can it be used in same game parlays?

### Takeaway
FanDuel lists the double-double as a yes-only player market (one "Yes" price per player; no "No" price was observed for any player on 2026-10-29), settles it on official NBA statistics including overtime, voids it only if the player never takes the court, and accepts it in parlays and same game parlays (it has run profit-boost promotions built around 2+ leg double-double parlays). FanDuel does not price every player, and on game morning it had not yet posted some players that other books had. The NBA's October 2026 integrity memo does not, as reported, restrict "Yes" double-double bets.

### Inferences
- The "No" side of a double-double is economically an under. FanDuel does not post it, and the 2026-27 memo (no unders on sub-12-minute players, no same-player under parlays) points the industry away from "No"-type positions on fringe players, although the reported memo does not name double-doubles.
- Because a double-double "Yes" stands once the player checks in, an early injury exit is a loss. Bet Protect+ converts that tail into a fixed 3% of stake, so it adds value only when the chance of an injury exit without return, before the double-double is reached, exceeds roughly 3% for that game. I found no injury-exit base rate to test this.
- Two-way and 10-day contract players are outside the prop menu under NBA guidance, so a double-double model universe should exclude them.

### Gaps
- FanDuel's current DC house rules were not readable (403; browser blocked). The rule text above is the Massachusetts filing dated 2023-08-24; DC wording may differ.
- How FanDuel's SGP engine prices a double-double combined with the same player's points or rebounds legs is not documented publicly.
- Posting-time pattern (night before versus game morning, and which players FanDuel skips) rests on one morning snapshot.

## 2. What do FanDuel double-double prices look like for near-locks versus 50/50 players, and what hold is embedded?

### Takeaway
Observed FanDuel prices: near-locks -700 to -1100 (87.5% to 91.7% implied); mid-tier bigs -170 to +140 (63.0% to 41.7%); fringe players up to +1280 (7.2%). On the two near-locks observed on 2026-10-28/29, FanDuel's "Yes" was shorter than every other listed book. Against de-vigged two-way prices from other books, FanDuel's one-sided margin on those two players was about 6% to 12% using multiplicative de-vig, 3% to 9% using additive or Shin, and 2% to 8% using the power method; expected value of the FanDuel "Yes" was about -2% to -11%. Two-way double-double markets elsewhere carried 5% to 9% overround, in line with two-way player props generally (7.93% average in an August 2026 cross-book panel).

### Inferences
- For a near-lock, the de-vig method choice moves the fair "Yes" probability by more than three percentage points (Jokic 86.5% multiplicative versus 90.2% power on the same BetMGM quote), which is larger than the edge most models claim. Any edge estimate on -500 or shorter prices should report results under at least two de-vig methods.
- The FanDuel milestone ladder offers an internal consistency check: Allen's double-double (41.7%) sat below FanDuel's own 10+ rebounds price (47.2%), and Duren's (63.0%) below his 10+ rebounds price (69.2%). For centers, P(double-double) is close to P(10+ points and 10+ rebounds), so a joint model of points and rebounds can be compared with FanDuel's ladder.
- On the two near-locks observed, a FanDuel-only bettor faced a shorter "Yes" than at any other listed book; a DC bettor can line-shop across the other DC books (Section 8).

### Gaps
- Only two FanDuel near-lock prices and one long shot were observed live alongside two-way quotes; mid-tier FanDuel prices come from research articles without matching two-way quotes. A real hold estimate needs a season of FanDuel "Yes" prices matched to two-way closes (Section 6 data).
- I found no published measurement of FanDuel's hold on one-way NBA props.

## 3. Does FanDuel offer a "No double-double" side, and how does one-sided pricing change edge measurement?

### Takeaway
No. On 2026-10-29 FanDuel showed "NL" (no line) for "No" on every player it priced. With only one side, FanDuel's price contains its margin but no observable overround, so edge must be measured against an external fair probability: a de-vigged two-way double-double price from another book or exchange, a cross-book consensus, or a model. CLV should be computed against that external de-vigged close, not only against FanDuel's own closing "Yes" price.

### Inferences
- Practical measurement: EV of a FanDuel "Yes" = p_fair x (FanDuel decimal odds) - 1, with p_fair from a two-way reference; FanDuel's one-sided margin = FanDuel implied probability / p_fair - 1. When no two-way reference exists for a player, the fallback is a model probability, or FanDuel's own milestone ladder adjusted for its vig.
- One-sided pricing removes the usual hold signal, so a FanDuel move with no move at two-way books is more informative about FanDuel's own risk position than about the true probability.

### Gaps
- No public evaluation of which two-way double-double source (theScore Bet, BetMGM, exchanges, consensus) predicts outcomes with less error than the others.
- Whether Pinnacle posts NBA double-double props today is asserted only by a vendor guide; I did not confirm it.

## 4. How do books generate double-double prices, how fast do they move on news, and how efficient is the market?

### Takeaway
FanDuel says it prices with an in-house global risk and trading team using statistical models and real-time data; no source ties its double-double prices to a third-party feed. Third-party prop pricers (Swish Analytics, Digital Sports Tech, Sportradar's odds feed, OpticOdds partners) exist and sell automated prop origination to operators, but I found no public mapping of US books to suppliers for double-doubles. Prices change through the day and books pull markets on news; observed open-to-morning moves ranged from small (Allen) to about seven probability points (Sabonis). No study of double-double market efficiency was found; practitioner commentary rates FanDuel as relatively efficient on props.

## 5. What limits and account restrictions does FanDuel apply to winning prop bettors, and what stakes are realistic?

### Takeaway
FanDuel's rules let it cut stakes per user at any time; it publishes no prop maximums. Regulator data from Massachusetts show about 0.64% of accounts limited (December 2024), most of them to 1% to 24% of the default stake, and that bettors who consistently beat the closing line are more likely to be limited. ## 9. How do practitioners measure CLV on binary props without a point line, which de-vig methods apply, and what CLV level indicates real edge?

### Takeaway
For yes/no markets, CLV is a price comparison: the probability implied by the price taken versus the de-vigged closing probability, or equivalently the percent improvement in decimal odds over the fair close. Because FanDuel's double-double has no "No" side, the closing fair probability must come from a two-way market elsewhere or from a consensus. Multiplicative (proportional) de-vig is the default but misallocates margin when prices are lopsided; margin-proportional-to-odds, power (logarithmic), odds-ratio, and Shin methods account for favorite-longshot bias and tested closer to zero bias in Buchdahl's football sample. Benchmarks for "real edge" are weak; the recent NBA evidence (2026) says CLV must exceed the margin to pay, and small positive CLV did not.

### Cited Findings
- [US][2026] Whelan measures CLV as "the percentage improvement in decimal odds" against the median closing quote across books; positive average CLV "does not guarantee that you will win long-term"; only the top CLV decile earned meaningful returns (about 11.4%); the ninth decile (about 5% CLV) returned 0.2%; for college basketball, early underdog bets often earn CLV but close at prices too poor to win. Source: [Whelan, "The Truth about Closing Line Value," 2026-08-12](https://www.karlwhelan.com/?p=2595).
- [US] Buchdahl tested de-vig methods on Pinnacle closing 1X2 football odds (136,876 games, 2007-08-11 to 2016-06-04); level-stakes yields at the de-vigged "true" odds: Pinnacle raw -3.36%, equal margin (proportional) -0.56%, margin proportional to odds +0.1%, odds ratio -0.20%, logarithmic function +0.07%. He attributes the equal-margin shortfall to the favourite-longshot bias, since it leaves longshot "true" prices too short. Source: [Buchdahl, "Using the Wisdom of the Crowd to Find Value in a Football Match Betting Market," updated version with data to May 2017](https://www.football-data.co.uk/The_Wisdom_of_the_Crowd_updated.pdf).
- [US] Power method: Clarke, Kovalchik and Ingram (2017) build on Vovk and Zhdanov (2009) and Clarke (2016), raising implied probabilities to a common power so they sum to one; it keeps probabilities within 0 to 1 and can capture the favourite-longshot bias. Source: [Clarke, Kovalchik and Ingram, American Journal of Sports Science 5(6), 2017](https://www.sciencepublishinggroup.com/article/10.11648/j.ajss.20170506.12) (search snippet). Shin's method (Shin 1992, 1993) solves for an insider-trading share; with two outcomes it equals the additive method. Source: [R package `implied` vignette](https://cran.nics.utk.edu/cran/web/packages/implied/vignettes/introduction.html) (search snippet); my calculations reproduced the equality (Section 2 figures).
- [US] On NFL player props, the power de-vig produced higher outcome correlations than multiplicative for every book tested. Source: [Chanchani, 2024-02-02](https://shaanchanchani.github.io/articles/sportsbook-props).
- [US] A Buchdahl article (mirrored copy) says that if you beat the closing no-vig price, your bets "should hold expected value," and that a 5% edge on even-money bets can take several thousand results-based bets to separate from chance. ### Inferences
- Recommended CLV definitions for a FanDuel double-double "Yes" (one-sided): (1) price CLV = FanDuel decimal taken / FanDuel decimal at close - 1 (vig-inclusive, measures movement only); (2) fair CLV = p_close_fair x FanDuel decimal taken - 1, with p_close_fair de-vigged from two-way closes (theScore Bet, BetMGM, Hard Rock, ProphetX, Kalshi when listed) or a consensus; report it under multiplicative and power methods. Fair CLV above zero is the edge test; price CLV alone cannot show edge because FanDuel's margin is not observable.
- [Calc] Method sensitivity is large at the extremes where double-double betting sits: for BetMGM Jokic -1100/+600 the fair "Yes" is 86.5% (multiplicative), 88.7% (additive or Shin), 90.2% (power); for theScore Bet D. Daniels +650/-1300 the fair "Yes" is 12.6%, 10.2%, 8.6%. A strategy that looks +3% under one method can be -3% under another.
- Benchmarks: given Whelan's finding that about 5% CLV returned roughly break-even on 4.5%-margin NBA moneylines, a FanDuel double-double "Yes" strategy should require fair CLV that clears FanDuel's one-sided margin (about 2% to 12% in the snapshot) before treating it as an edge, and should be judged on fair CLV over several hundred bets with bootstrap intervals rather than on win rate.

### Gaps
- No published CLV methodology or benchmark specific to yes/no player props or double-doubles.
- No published comparison of de-vig methods on NBA player props; the method evidence comes from football 1X2 (Buchdahl) and NFL props (Chanchani).
