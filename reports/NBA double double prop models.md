# Price, not prediction, decides double-double bets

No public NBA double-double model has a verified out-of-sample betting record: the double-double-specific evidence is a vendor's self-reported 12-4 run over 16 picks ([Dimers](https://www.dimers.com/nba/news/2025-nba-betting-how-to-capitalize-on-the-leagues-historical-triple-double-numbers)) and one open-source backtest in which double-double bets returned -26% on a blind season ([KyShiesty README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KyShiesty919/NBA-Props/main/README.md)), so what works has to be read from adjacent projection and prop research. That research converges on one design: a separate minutes forecast, long-window per-minute rates shrunk toward a prior, stats drawn jointly given minutes, recalibrated probabilities, and the de-vigged market price as the benchmark. In the one prop model that measured it, the market alone scored a better Brier score than the model (0.244 vs 0.248) and a blend of the two did better still (0.241) ([KyShiesty README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KyShiesty919/NBA-Props/main/README.md)). For a bot that bets two or three double-doubles a night, the parts worth taking are a minutes distribution with a blowout branch tied to the spread, joint stat draws, isotonic recalibration in the 0.6 to 0.95 band, and a de-vigged two-way price logged beside every pick; usage-redistribution bets, defense-versus-position adjustments and tracking features have no measured lift yet. Box scores, play-by-play and NBA.com tracking aggregates (rebound chances, potential assists, box outs) are free, with per-game tracking self-built at about 1,000 stats.nba.com calls a season ([nba_stats_tracking](https://github.com/dblackrun/nba-stats-tracking/blob/master/nba_stats_tracking/tracking.py)), but historical double-double prices are not free: The Odds API archive starts in May 2023 on paid plans ([The Odds API](https://the-odds-api.com/liveapi/guides/v4/)). On beating FanDuel, the evidence points the other way for the near-lock tier the bot would buy: FanDuel lists the market Yes-only, and its -1100 on Nikola Jokic and -700 on Domantas Sabonis in late October carried about -2% to -11% expected value against de-vigged two-way quotes at other books ([BettingPros](https://www.bettingpros.com/nba/odds/player-props/double-double/)) and imply hit rates of 91.7% and 87.5%, above the 80% to 87% per-game rates of the top double-double producers in 2024-25 and above every high-volume player's 2025-26 rate, when Jokic led at 84.6% ([Land of Basketball](https://www.landofbasketball.com/year_by_year_stats/2025_2026_double_doubles_rs.htm)). At prices from -250 to -700, a real 2 to 3 point edge would take roughly 900 to 3,950 bets to confirm at 80% power (about 2 to 12 seasons), and once FanDuel trims stakes, which Massachusetts data show happens disproportionately to closing-line beaters ([Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/massachusetts-limiting-sports-betting-prediction-markets-exchanges-sharps-box-september-2025)), it would be worth roughly $190 to $2,100 a season at $25 to $100 caps (computed). The strikeout season's lesson applies with higher stakes: the 80.1% floor-strategy hit rate that was roughly break-even to +7% at -300 to -400 would lose about 8.5% of stake at -700 (computed), so this bot should select on fair edge against the top available price and log a two-way fair close from the first night.

*Evidence labels.* Figures carry one of five tags: **measured** (computed from data by a third party, a regulator or a published study), **self-reported** (an author's or vendor's claim about its own model, picks or backtest, not independently checked), **asserted** (a claim with no reported test), **computed** (arithmetic on cited inputs, by the research team or by me), and **excerpt** (seen only in a search-engine summary because the page could not be opened). Peer review is noted where it applies. References to the project mean the Double Double Bot folder; its files are local evidence, not published sources.

## The public double-double record is 16 vendor bets and one losing backtest

The research team found no peer-reviewed paper that predicts NBA double-doubles, and what exists is thin. A Towards Data Science tutorial fits a logistic regression for one player, Nikola Vucevic, and drops the five component stats from the predictors to avoid leakage, but its accuracy figures could not be retrieved ([Towards Data Science](https://towardsdatascience.com/how-to-predict-nba-double-doubles-f4c30be08ca0/), excerpt). A Russell Westbrook triple-double capstone used same-game minutes and plus/minus as predictors, which explains games after the fact rather than forecasting them, and reports no accuracy metrics ([bookdown capstone](https://bookdown.org/theqdata/data_science_capstone_project_report/conclusion-and-future-work.html)). Dimers claimed in February 2025 that its double-double predictor went 12-4 for +5.55 units on flat one-unit bets (self-reported). That implies an average winning return near 0.80 units, about -126 odds (computed), so the picks sat near even money, not at the -400 to -1100 prices a top-of-the-board bot buys ([Dimers](https://www.dimers.com/nba/news/2025-nba-betting-how-to-capitalize-on-the-leagues-historical-triple-double-numbers)). The one double-double backtest found lists double-doubles at **-26% on a blind 2025-26 season** with no sample size, in a table where the row above attributes a -40% loss on alternate points ladders to longshot bias ([KyShiesty README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KyShiesty919/NBA-Props/main/README.md), self-reported).

The more rigorous public work targets single stats, and its honest holdouts tell one story: development edges shrink or vanish out of sample. One repository's rule of betting other books when they disagreed with FanDuel's line by 3 or more probability points returned **+6.1% on 6,424 development bets and -1.8% on a 35-bet pre-registered holdout**, and its README says no edge has been proven ([seantanger](https://github.com/seantanger/nba-props-research), self-reported). Another found a gradient-boosted overs model at +18% in-sample and -6% blind, and its author concludes that none of his tested strategies is profitable at statistical significance ([KyShiesty](https://github.com/KyShiesty919/NBA-Props), self-reported). A University of Malta thesis found a transformer more accurate than recurrent alternatives but still short of bookmaker accuracy and unprofitable ([OAR@UM](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/handle/123456789/115275)). Accuracy gains over naive baselines are small. One hobbyist model cut points MAE from 4.634 to 4.547 on about 103,000 player-games per stat, while its author estimates that knowing actual minutes would cut MAE by 9% to 28% ([GitHub PR #49](https://github.com/lmaynor/mlb-betting/pull/49), self-reported). A peer-reviewed evaluation of 1,658 paid fantasy projections from four providers found the forecasts inefficient, with bias in some cases ([Koehler, Tegtmeier and Doepke 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12197-023-09646-7), measured). That is the shape of your 2026 strikeout audit, where LightGBM tied the baseline and the posted line beat the model on error (MAE 1.90 vs 2.04).

Track records do not fill the gap. No NBA prop model or capper has a public, third-party-verified, multi-season, prop-only record with closing-line value (CLV). The verifiable records are one-year, all-NBA leaderboards synced to sportsbook accounts, with ROI from -0.2% to +20.8% on page one and no CLV or settled sample sizes ([Juice Reel](https://www.juicereel.com/best-nba-handicappers), measured by account sync), and 30-day employer-tracked records of 6 to 85 picks ([Action Network](https://www.actionnetwork.com/nba/picks/top-experts), self-reported by the platform). Selection alone manufactures winners: among 100 bettors with zero edge at -300, about 14 to 19 would finish a 330 to 495 bet season 2 points above break-even (computed, exact binomial).

Read together, the evidence makes the sportsbook price the forecaster to beat. A pricing supplier describes simulating player props and then applying hold and limits ([Huddle](https://huddle.tech/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/NBA-Player-Proposition-Micro-Markets-From-Creation-to-Resulting.pdf), excerpt). FanDuel says an in-house trading team prices with statistical models and real-time data ([FanDuel FAQ](https://www.fanduel.com/about/news/faqs), self-reported). The one prop model that compared itself with the de-vigged market lost that comparison. A successful public model, on this record, is one whose structure survives honest validation and whose probabilities stay calibrated against the price.

## Minutes carry the news while per-minute rates carry the skill

Every documented system forecasts playing time separately from per-minute production and treats playing time as the hard part. DARKO, a public daily projection system, says no projection input is harder to forecast than minutes, and it reports beating two DFS projection sites on every stat it tested except minutes, without publishing error figures ([DARKO](https://www.darko.app/about), self-reported). It splits playing time into playing at all, starting role and minutes role, with decay half-lives of about 3, 1 and 2 games, so the last few games and the news feed dominate ([DARKO](https://www.darko.app/about)). The only public game-level benchmark found is a student LightGBM model on 151,493 player-games from 2012 to 2018: **test MSE 38.24 and R-squared 0.65, against 50.24 and 0.55 for a five-game average** ([UBC-MDS](https://github.com/UBC-MDS/NBA-Minutes-Predictor), measured, student project). That is an RMSE near 6.2 minutes, about 19% of a 32-minute projection and of every count stat that scales with it; for a center collecting 0.35 rebounds a minute, a six-minute shortfall costs about two rebounds, often the gap between a double-double and a miss (computed, illustrative).

Per-minute skill is the stable half. Against a league-average prior, rebounds per 100 possessions need only 60.52 possessions of padding and assists per 100 need 55.57, roughly one game of a starter's court time, in a fit on about 750,000 rows since 2001 ([Medvedovsky](https://kmedved.com/2020/08/06/nba-stabilization-rates-and-the-padding-approach/), measured). Among 1,866 player-seasons of players who changed roles, total rebound rate correlated 0.950 and assist rate 0.906 with the player's own prior season, while usage correlated 0.824 with his own prior and 0.032 with his predecessor's ([Paine, Basketball-Reference blog](https://www.basketball-reference.com/blog/index1540.html?p=9776), measured, 1978 to 2011 data). DARKO's fitted half-lives run about 24 games for assist rate, 46 for defensive rebounds and 54 for offensive rebound rate ([DARKO](https://www.darko.app/about), self-reported). Franks and co-authors found rebounds, blocks and assists both discriminative and stable, with per-minute metrics steadier than totals because injuries move totals ([Franks et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1609.09830), preprint, measured).

The split works because the two halves move on different clocks. Skill changes over months, so a long decay window shrunk toward a prior estimates it well. Playing time changes with tonight's injury report, rest decisions, foul trouble and score margin, so a short window plus news has to carry it, and separating the two lets the fast input update without re-estimating skill. The adjustments show where the news sits. Over-prop hit rates fell from **46.7% in games decided by 7 or fewer points to 40.9% in games decided by 20 or more** across 40,856 graded props ([DataStreak](https://datastreak.com/insights/nba-blowouts-4th-quarter-props), measured, seasons and books not stated). In one backtest, a blind-season result reached z 2.9: unders in games with spreads of 10 or more returned +20.9% on 190 bets, which the author attributes to starters sitting in blowouts ([KyShiesty README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KyShiesty919/NBA-Props/main/README.md), self-reported). A common DFS rule cuts projected minutes 1.5% for each point of spread beyond 7 ([RotoGrinders](https://rotogrinders.com/fantasy/lessons/accurately-predicting-minutes-nba-dfs), asserted rule of thumb). Second nights of back-to-backs move team efficiency, rebounding and pace by about 0.5% to 2% ([Aicardi and Bailey, via Economist Writing Every Day](https://economistwritingeveryday.com/2026/07/09/do-nba-teams-play-worse-in-back-to-back-games/), measured, team level). Per-36 points, rebounds and assists did not change significantly after long absences in a 20-case study ([Statathlon](https://statathlon.com/evaluating-the-effect-of-mid-season-absences/), measured, small sample), so a return from injury is a minutes event, as with Victor Wembanyama's 21 minutes on a restriction against a season average near 34 ([Yahoo Sports](https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/victor-wembanyama-minutes-restriction-know-090001689.html)).

A double-double is an AND event across five categories (points, rebounds, assists, steals, blocks), and shared minutes push the categories up and down together, so multiplying marginal probabilities understates it. One open-source tool reports that independence under-rated triple-doubles by roughly half before a logistic correction ([JoshuaDHaber PR #16](https://github.com/JoshuaDHaber/NFL-Prop-Predictor/pull/16), self-reported). Another fit a Gaussian copula over 302 stat pairs and, on 2025-26 DraftKings play-in and playoff lines, predicted 26.17% for combinations that hit 26.00% of the time, against 23.79% under independence ([DEMON-X13 PR #4](https://github.com/DEMON-X13/nba-hub/pull/4), self-reported). How much this matters depends on the tier. The Frechet bounds hold P(A and B) between P(A) + P(B) - 1 and the smaller of the two, so a center at 0.97 to score 10 and 0.85 to grab 10 rebounds sits between **0.82 and 0.85** under any dependence, while a fringe candidate at 0.70 and 0.60 can sit anywhere from **0.30 to 0.60** (computed). For near-locks, the work is the second category's marginal (usually rebounds) and the left tail of minutes. For mid-tier players the joint model decides the answer, and players with three live paths, as Jokic has, need every pair counted. No published source gives within-player correlations of points, rebounds and assists, so they have to be estimated from game logs.

Count shape also matters near the threshold. A Poisson rebound mean of 9 gives a 41.3% chance of 10 or more (computed), and extra dispersion raises that chance when the mean sits below 10 and lowers it above 10. NBA team scoring is mildly overdispersed, with a negative binomial beating normal and Poisson fits on leave-one-out comparison ([PMC8282683](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8282683/), measured, peer-reviewed). Vendors and pricing suppliers simulate stat lines rather than classify the double-double directly ([SaberSim](https://support.sabersim.com/en/articles/12079141-building-lineups), excerpt; [Huddle](https://huddle.tech/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/NBA-Player-Proposition-Micro-Markets-From-Creation-to-Resulting.pdf), excerpt), and DARKO multiplies per-possession rates by projected minutes and pace ([DARKO](https://www.darko.app/about)). No study compares a direct double-double classifier with a simulated joint model on the same games. A direct classifier trained on elite players with 80% to 87% rates mostly relearns the season rate unless it sees minutes-risk inputs such as the spread, rest, foul rate and teammate absences (inference, untested).

Calibration and the market close the loop. In NBA game betting, choosing models by calibration rather than accuracy returned **+34.69% ROI on average against -35.17%** ([Walsh and Joshi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06021), preprint, backtest). The KyShiesty prop model's original settings were overconfident by 5 to 11 points in every probability bucket, and its realized hit rates landed about 1 point above the market's fair price, in the author's words "a real edge, smaller than the vig" ([KyShiesty README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KyShiesty919/NBA-Props/main/README.md), self-reported). Its Brier scores were 0.248 for the model, 0.244 for the de-vigged market and 0.241 for a blend that weights the market at 0.70 when a player has no games yet that season and 0.35 after about 15 ([KyShiesty](https://github.com/KyShiesty919/NBA-Props), self-reported). A peer-reviewed NBA game model gained profit by penalizing correlation with bookmaker probabilities ([Hubacek, Sourek and Zelezny 2019](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:intfor:v:35:y:2019:i:2:p:783-796)), and a peer-reviewed study found that opening NBA lines carried significant errors when a high-quality player was absent but offered no profitable strategy at the close ([Dare, Dennis and Paul 2015](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v13y2015icp130-136.html)). A model earns money only where it disagrees with an informed price and is right, which makes this a decorrelation problem rather than an accuracy problem. Favorites also punish calibration error: at -525 each point of probability error moves expected ROI by about 1.2 points, so a 5 to 11 point overconfidence turns a break-even bet into an apparent 6% to 13% edge (computed).

## Five parts earn a place in the bot, and the market price is one

The bot's stated rule, the top two or three players by double-double probability each night, selects the bets the market already prices near certainty. Ranking by probability concentrates stakes at -500 and shorter, where FanDuel's one-sided margin in the observed snapshot ran up to about 12% (see the FanDuel section below) and where the choice of de-vig method alone moves the fair probability by more than three points ([BettingPros Jokic](https://www.bettingpros.com/nba/props/nikola-jokic/double-double/), computed). The pool is also thin. In 2024-25, Sabonis (87.1%), Jokic (84.3%), Giannis Antetokounmpo (82.1%) and Karl-Anthony Towns (80.6%) cleared 80% per game played; in 2025-26 only Jokic (84.6%) did among high-volume players, and injuries held Sabonis, Antetokounmpo and Anthony Davis to 19, 36 and 20 games ([Land of Basketball 2024-25](https://www.landofbasketball.com/year_by_year_stats/2024_2025_double_doubles_rs.htm); [2025-26](https://www.landofbasketball.com/year_by_year_stats/2025_2026_double_doubles_rs.htm), measured, rates computed, single-site counts not yet cross-checked against Basketball-Reference). Three independent picks at 85% all hit on 61% of nights; at 2025-26's top three rates (84.6%, 74.7%, 68.1%) they all hit on 43% (computed). The rule the evidence supports is the top two or three by fair edge against the top available price, with a minimum edge above the book's margin. The parts below serve that rule.

Three popular inputs fail the evidence test for version one. Injury-driven usage redistribution is real but already priced: the KyShiesty model's usage-based bets returned -3.2% against +0.7% without them, and boosted players still came in about 0.9 points high ([KyShiesty](https://github.com/KyShiesty919/NBA-Props), self-reported), consistent with closing lines absorbing absences ([Dare, Dennis and Paul](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v13y2015icp130-136.html)). Absences belong inside the minutes model, and a standalone absence angle pays only if the bot acts before the price moves. For rebounds the redistribution has a mechanical basis: older estimates put roughly 0.5 to 0.9 of a player's rebounds as ones a teammate would otherwise collect, so a missing center's defensive rebounds flow mostly to the remaining bigs ([Birnbaum](http://blog.philbirnbaum.com/2011/01/do-players-steal-rebounding.html), measured, pre-2018 data, excerpt). Defense versus position was judged largely noise by a DFS site that backtested it without publishing numbers ([Fantasy Team Advice](https://fantasyteamadvice.com/nba/dvp), asserted), and no published study quantifies how opponent rebounding or assist allowances move an individual's counts. Small measured multipliers are safe to carry. Team pace after 4 games explains half the variance of its season-end value ([Nylon Calculus](https://fansided.com/2017/12/21/nylon-calculus-team-stats-noise-stabilization-thunder/), measured), and home teams with fans averaged 1.08 more defensive rebounds and 1.02 more assists per game in 2019-20, a gap that vanished in the empty arenas of 2020-21 ([Lu et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.951779/full), measured, peer-reviewed).

Two operating rules follow from the same evidence. Near-locks in games with spreads of 10 or more need extra edge or a pass, given the blowout results above. The first weeks of a season need caution: in one backtest, bets in each season's first five games ran -28% ([KyShiesty](https://github.com/KyShiesty919/NBA-Props), self-reported), and the 2026-27 season opened October 20 ([Dimers](https://www.dimers.com/nba/news/nba-player-props-bans-regulated-betting-2026-27)), so the bot starts in the window where priors carry heavy weight.

The v1 spec already in the project (a direct LightGBM classifier with isotonic calibration, a shrunk season-rate baseline, switchable teammate-absence features, and top-N hit-rate metrics) covers rates, recalibration and the mid-tier slice. The research points to four changes. First, use the de-vigged market as the baseline for the seasons with price history, since the price, not a season rate, is what the bot has to beat. Second, score expected value and fair CLV at logged prices rather than top-N hit rates, which reward buying short prices. Third, add a minutes distribution with a blowout branch keyed to the spread; the spec's point-differential proxy for expected margin is a free stand-in, not the same input. Fourth, treat the absence features as honest only if the bot bets after inactives are final. Starting lineups are typically confirmed about 30 minutes before tip ([Fantasy Team Advice](https://fantasyteamadvice.com/nba/starting-lineups), excerpt), and a backtest that feeds a morning bet the realized inactive list shows accuracy no pregame model can reach ([GitHub PR #49](https://github.com/lmaynor/mlb-betting/pull/49), self-reported).

## Box scores and tracking are free to backtest; double-double prices are not

Everything needed to backtest the minutes, rate and joint-distribution parts is free. The project already holds ESPN box scores for 2017-18 through 2026-27 through sportsdataverse (project log, 2026-10-29). NBA.com's stats endpoints, reached through the MIT-licensed `nba_api` Python client with no key, cover game logs, box scores, play-by-play and shot charts, but the maintainers warn that stats.nba.com slows or drops rapid requests and often does not answer cloud providers' IP ranges ([nba_api](https://github.com/swar/nba_api)). Basketball-Reference has no API and blocks sessions that exceed 20 requests a minute for up to a day ([Sports Reference](https://www.sports-reference.com/bot-traffic.html)). pbpstats offers a free play-by-play API ([pbpstats](https://pbpstats.readthedocs.io/)), and the shufinskiy/nba_data repository packages play-by-play from 1996-97 and matchup data from 2017-18 to 2024-25 as downloadable files, last updated February 2025 ([shufinskiy/nba_data](https://github.com/shufinskiy/nba_data)). A 2026 backtest used NBA.com's historical daily lineups to identify actual starters ([Eth91 PR #114](https://github.com/Eth91/tennis-odds-collector/pull/114), self-reported), and one prop-research repository built injury features from NBA official injury reports ([seantanger](https://github.com/seantanger/nba-props-research)), so historical lineup and injury sources exist, though their formats were not reviewed. Pregame spreads for the blowout branch are the exception: The Odds API's featured-market history starts in June 2020 on paid plans ([The Odds API v4 docs](https://the-odds-api.com/liveapi/guides/v4/)), and the spec's point-differential proxy is the free substitute.

Tracking, the motion layer, is free in aggregate and has to be assembled per game. League-wide optical tracking began with SportVU in 2013-14, moved to Second Spectrum in 2017-18 and to Sony's Hawk-Eye with Sportradar in 2023-24 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Player_tracking_%28National_Basketball_Association%29); [NBA PR](https://pr.nba.com/nba-announces-multiyear-partnership-sportradar-second-spectrum/); [ESPN](https://www.espn.com/nba/story/_/id/35818363/nba-use-hawk-eye-tracking-system-follow-players-ball)), and hustle stats such as box outs and deflections start with the 2016 playoffs ([NBA.com](https://www.nba.com/news/2022-23-nba-hustle-award)). Two per-game endpoints are still served. `boxscoreplayertrackv3` carries rebound chances, touches, passes, secondary and free-throw assists, speed and distance ([nba_api docs](https://github.com/swar/nba_api/blob/master/docs/nba_api/stats/endpoints/boxscoreplayertrackv3.md)); `boxscorehustlev2` carries box outs, deflections, screen assists and loose balls ([nba_api docs](https://github.com/swar/nba_api/blob/master/docs/nba_api/stats/endpoints/boxscorehustlev2.md)). Potential assists, contested and deferred rebounds, adjusted rebound chance percentage, time of possession and drives exist only in date-range dashboards. Setting the start and end date to the same day returns one game per player, which Darryl Blackport's `nba_stats_tracking` package automates with a two-second pause per request ([nba_stats_tracking](https://github.com/dblackrun/nba-stats-tracking/blob/master/nba_stats_tracking/tracking.py)). That route takes about 1,000 calls a season and about 9,000 for 2017-18 through 2025-26, one or two overnight runs at 20 to 30 calls a minute from a residential IP (computed by the research team; no official rate limit is published). No free per-game tracking dump was found. Raw player coordinates exist publicly only for about 631 games of 2015-16 SportVU data ([PeerJ preprint](https://peerj.com/preprints/3201v1.pdf)), and Hawk-Eye pose data and Synergy video are commercial products ([Sportico](https://www.sportico.com/business/tech/2023/nba-player-tracking-second-spectrum-dragon-1234711887/); [Sportradar](https://investors.sportradar.com/news-releases/news-release-details/sportradar-acquires-synergy-sports)).

Reliability is the practical cost. The `nba_api` changelog records header fixes in September 2025, February 2026 and October 2026, the removal of the V2 player-tracking box score after server errors, five V2 box scores listed as no longer served, and team game logs deprecated by the NBA with no replacement ([nba_api CHANGELOG](https://github.com/swar/nba_api/blob/master/CHANGELOG.MD)). An issue opened in August 2026 reports the tracking dashboard returning zero rows for 2014 to 2026 pulls, with a season-format fix suggested ([nba_api issue #688](https://github.com/swar/nba_api/issues/688)). Users report that requests from CI runners and cloud hosts time out ([Stack Overflow mirror](https://kiwix.lema.org/stackoverflow.com_en_all_2023-11/questions/73066785/nba-api-timeouts-when-run-with-github-workflow), self-reported by users). In this project, stats.nba.com already timed out on season-wide calls, and installing `nba_api` is an open dependency decision (project log, 2026-10-29).

The data also changes definition underneath a multi-season model. SportVU-era wording on NBA.com counted a rebound chance when a player was within 3.5 feet of the rebound ([Space City Scoop](https://spacecityscoop.com/2015/08/28/nba-player-stats-top-5-players-rebounding)); the current glossary credits the player who is closest to the ball once it drops below the rim ([NBA.com glossary](https://www.nba.com/stats/help/glossary)). One analyst reports that box-out measurement changed sharply after 2018-19 ([A Jump Shot in the Dark](https://jumpshotinthedark.com/2023/07/03/quantifying-the-nbas-most-impactful-rebounders-using-tracking-data/), asserted), and no published audit checks rebound chances, touches or potential assists for a level shift at the 2023-24 Hawk-Eye switch. Box-score targets do not change with vendors, but tracking features do, so 2017-18, 2019-20 and 2023-24 are candidate breakpoints; about 65 team-level calls would test them (computed by the research team).

Whether tracking improves a double-double forecast is untested. No published study measures whether rebound chances, contested rebound share, box outs, potential assists or touches improve next-game rebound or assist forecasts after controlling for minutes and past per-minute rates, and claims that rebound chances beat raw averages for props are asserted ([The Wager Theorem](https://thewagertheorem.com/nba-prop-bet-stats-better-than-points/)). The measured evidence is indirect. Box-score rates already correlate 0.91 to 0.95 season to season ([Paine](https://www.basketball-reference.com/blog/index1540.html?p=9776)), which leaves little room at the season level. Tracking-built rebounding models are far steadier year to year (R-squared 0.60 to 0.77) than ridge-regressed rebounding impact (0.10 to 0.16), but that target is team rebounding impact, not a player's box-score rebounds ([A Jump Shot in the Dark](https://jumpshotinthedark.com/2023/07/03/quantifying-the-nbas-most-impactful-rebounders-using-tracking-data/), measured). About one in three offensive rebound contests involved two teammates chasing the same ball ([Nylon Calculus](https://fansided.com/2016/04/05/deep-dives-investigating-rebound-tradeoffs), measured, 2014-15), so rebound chances partly measure overlap rather than opportunity. The large and persistent measured effect is on assists: in 2015-16 the home scorekeeper's bonus ranged from **-3.44 assists per team-game in Utah to +2.32 in New Orleans** on a 22.05 average, and scorekeeper generosity correlated 0.78 to 0.89 across seasons ([van Bommel and Bornn](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1602.08754), measured, peer-reviewed). Tracking fits a version-two experiment: decayed per-minute rates times projected minutes as the baseline, then the same model plus per-minute rebound chances, potential assists and box outs from prior games only, scored by rolling-origin log loss, with any gain expected early in the season and after role changes (hypothesis).

Prices are the binding data constraint, and they are what the strikeout season never logged. The research found no free archive of historical double-double prices; one vendor claims free historical prop odds, unverified ([OddsPapi](https://oddspapi.io/blog/?p=2824), self-reported). The Odds API documents `player_double_double` as a Yes/No market served one event at a time ([The Odds API markets](https://the-odds-api.com/sports-odds-data/betting-markets.html)), and the project's own pull on the morning of October 29 returned 27 FanDuel and 28 DraftKings prices, every one on the Yes side (project file `data/odds/dd_prices.csv`). Because both books returned only Yes prices, that log cannot yet produce a de-vigged fair price. theScore Bet posted two-way double-double prices on several players that morning ([BettingPros grid](https://www.bettingpros.com/nba/odds/player-props/double-double/)), it sits in The Odds API's us2 region under the key `espnbet`, and up to 10 named bookmakers bill as one region ([bookmaker list](https://the-odds-api.com/sports-odds-data/bookmaker-apis.html); [v4 docs](https://the-odds-api.com/liveapi/guides/v4/)), so adding two-way books costs nothing extra per call while the list stays at 10 or fewer. Player-prop history starts 2023-05-03 at five-minute snapshots, costs 10 credits per market per region per event call, and sits on paid plans from $30 to $249 a month, though the pricing page and the docs disagree on whether the free tier can reach it ([v4 docs](https://the-odds-api.com/liveapi/guides/v4/); [pricing](https://the-odds-api.com/)). Two snapshots per game for 2023-24 through 2025-26 come to about 73,800 credits, one month of the $59 plan, and live collection at four polls a game is about 4,920 credits a season, above the free tier's 500 a month (computed). Whether FanDuel's double-double market sits in the 2023 to 2026 archive is unverified; one historical call would settle it. SportsDataIO sells props history from 2020 on quote ([SportsDataIO](https://sportsdata.io/historical-odds)), and one research repository sourced its historical prop prices from BettingPros ([seantanger](https://github.com/seantanger/nba-props-research)).

## FanDuel prices near-locks above what the top tier has delivered

### A Yes-only listing hides a one-sided margin of about 2% to 12%

FanDuel lists the double-double as a Yes-only market. BettingPros showed no FanDuel No line for any player on October 29 ([BettingPros grid](https://www.bettingpros.com/nba/odds/player-props/double-double/), measured, one morning), and the project's API pull agrees. Bets stand once the player takes the court, overtime counts, and bets void only if the player never plays ([FanDuel House Rules, Massachusetts filing](https://massgaming.com/wp-content/uploads/FanDuel-House-Rules-8.24.23.pdf)). An early injury exit is therefore a loss unless the bettor pays the 3% Bet Protect+ fee, which refunds a straight bet when the player leaves injured and does not return ([Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/fanduel-bet-protect-plus-full-game-injury-protection-nba-playoffs)). The NBA's 2026-27 restrictions on partner books, as reported, do not touch double-double Yes bets ([Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/nba-bans-certain-bet-types-ahead-of-new-season-oct-16-2026)). A practitioner newsletter argues that one-way prop markets let FanDuel price the single side less generously because no one can bet the other side against it ([BetSmart](https://betsmart.beehiiv.com/p/efficient-prop-markets), asserted). With one side posted, FanDuel's margin cannot be read from its own board, and one de-vig calculator refuses to de-vig one-sided books ([oddsguy.ai](https://oddsguy.ai/tools/devig-calculator), excerpt), so the margin has to be measured against an outside fair price. The two observed near-locks allow that.

| | Jokic, DEN at NOR (listed Oct. 28) | Sabonis, SAC at NOR (Oct. 29) |
|---|---|---|
| FanDuel Yes (break-even) | -1100 (91.7%) | -700 (87.5%) |
| Other listed books | DraftKings -500; BetMGM -1100/+600; theScore Bet -1000/+450; Hard Rock -650/+425; BetRivers -1115 | DraftKings -438; theScore Bet -550/+375; Hard Rock -475; ProphetX -400 |
| Fair Yes from two-way quotes, multiplicative to power method | 82.0% to 90.2% | 80.1% to 83.0% |
| Expected value of FanDuel's Yes | -1.7% to -10.6% | -5.2% to -8.5% |
| Realized double-double rate per game played | 84.3% (2024-25); 84.6% (2025-26) | 87.1% (2024-25); 63.2% (2025-26, 19 games) |

Sources: [BettingPros Jokic](https://www.bettingpros.com/nba/props/nikola-jokic/double-double/) and [Sabonis](https://www.bettingpros.com/nba/props/domantas-sabonis/double-double/) pages viewed 2026-10-29 (measured); DraftKings -438 on Sabonis from the project's 10:23 ET Odds API snapshot; realized rates from [Land of Basketball](https://www.landofbasketball.com/year_by_year_stats/2024_2025_double_doubles_rs.htm) (rates computed); fair prices and expected values computed.

FanDuel's near-lock prices sat at or near the short end of the board. Sabonis at -700 was shorter than every other listed book. Jokic at -1100 matched BetMGM, was a shade longer than BetRivers at -1115, and was far shorter than DraftKings at -500 and Hard Rock at -650 ([BettingPros Jokic](https://www.bettingpros.com/nba/props/nikola-jokic/double-double/), measured). Against de-vigged two-way quotes, FanDuel's implied one-sided margin on the two players was about 6% to 12% by the multiplicative method and 2% to 8% by the power method (computed). Two-way double-double markets elsewhere carried 5.2% to 9.1% overround (computed from the same pages), in line with a measured 7.93% average margin on two-sided player props across 52 bookmakers, against 4.77% on main lines ([Mendeley margin panel](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/yt3xsvk7zn/1), measured). The Yes side is also structurally an over: across 375,339 NBA props graded at closing prices, overs returned about -8.7% and unders -3.8% ([Sickfade](https://sickfade.com/overs-vs-unders), excerpt, method unverified). FanDuel is not a slow book on NBA props in general. A lead-lag analysis placed FanDuel (1.007) among five books with little separation, alongside DraftKings (1.046) and Circa (1.094) ([Pikkit](https://pikkit.com/blog/which-sportsbooks-are-sharp), measured, sample undisclosed), and a practitioner newsletter calls FanDuel's prop lines efficient ([BetSmart](https://betsmart.beehiiv.com/p/efficient-prop-markets), asserted).

The de-vig method matters as much as the model. On BetMGM's -1100/+600 Jokic quote, the fair Yes is 86.5% by the multiplicative method, 88.7% by the additive method and 90.2% by the power method (computed), a spread wider than the edge a good model claims. In Joseph Buchdahl's test on 136,876 Pinnacle football closes, proportional de-vig left a -0.56% yield while the logarithmic, odds-ratio and margin-proportional methods came within 0.2% of zero, which he attributes to favorite-longshot bias ([Buchdahl](https://www.football-data.co.uk/The_Wisdom_of_the_Crowd_updated.pdf), measured). On 12,624 NFL props, the power method tracked outcomes better than the multiplicative method at every book tested ([Chanchani](https://shaanchanchani.github.io/articles/sportsbook-props), measured). Every edge estimate should be reported under both methods, and a bet that is positive under only one should be treated as no edge.

In credit terms, a -700 Yes is a one-night note that pays 14.3 cents per dollar and loses the dollar on a miss, so the price implies a 12.5% miss rate; -1100 implies 8.3% (computed). The top producers' realized miss rates per game played over the last two seasons ran about 13% (Sabonis, 2024-25) to 16% (Jokic, both seasons) ([Land of Basketball 2024-25](https://www.landofbasketball.com/year_by_year_stats/2024_2025_double_doubles_rs.htm); [2025-26](https://www.landofbasketball.com/year_by_year_stats/2025_2026_double_doubles_rs.htm), rates computed). Nightly prices carry matchup and injury information that a season rate does not, so this comparison is suggestive rather than decisive. It agrees with the two-way books, though: on the nights observed, the coupon on FanDuel's near-locks did not cover expected loss.

### Hit rates need about 2 to 12 seasons to confirm a real edge

Even a genuine edge is slow to show up in results. The table uses the research team's one-sample test against break-even at 95% two-sided confidence; the -700 row applies the same formula (computed).

| Price | Break-even | ROI if the true rate is 2 points above break-even | Bets to confirm a 2-point edge, 50% / 80% power | Seasons at 3 picks a night, 80% power |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| -250 | 71.4% | +2.8% | 1,874 / 3,952 | 8.0 |
| -300 | 75.0% | +2.7% | 1,701 / 3,618 | 7.3 |
| -400 | 80.0% | +2.5% | 1,418 / 3,066 | 6.2 |
| -700 | 87.5% | +2.3% | 903 / 2,053 | 4.1 |

Seasons assume about 165 game nights, an assumption rather than a sourced count. A 3-point edge cuts the counts roughly in half: 1,593 bets at -300 and 890 at -700 for 80% power, or 3.2 and 1.8 seasons at three picks a night (computed). Near -300, one season cannot separate a 2 to 3 point edge from zero, because the 95% interval on a hit rate near 75% is about plus or minus 4.7 points at 330 bets and 3.8 at 495 (computed). Closing-line value is the faster test. Secondary summaries of Buchdahl's work put the per-bet standard deviation of CLV near 0.10, against about 1.00 for profit and loss at even money ([Pinnacle Odds Dropper](https://www.pinnacleoddsdropper.com/blog/closing-line-value--clv-demystified-by-expert-joseph-buchdahl), secondary summary), and one vendor estimates that a 2% edge at even money needs about 6,800 results-based bets but about 70 measured by CLV ([HowProsBet](https://howprosbet.com/how-to-verify-your-betting-edge/), asserted).

For a Yes-only market the CLV has to be fair CLV: the de-vigged two-way closing probability times FanDuel's decimal price taken, minus one, computed under both de-vig methods. FanDuel's own closing Yes price measures movement but cannot show edge, because its margin is not observable. Positive CLV is also not enough. On 3,670 NBA games from 2022-23 to 2024-25, moneylines showed no favorite-longshot bias and a 4.5% margin; bets in the ninth CLV decile averaged about 5% CLV and returned 0.2%, and only the top decile earned meaningful returns, about 11.4% ([Whelan](https://www.karlwhelan.com/?p=2595), measured). A FanDuel double-double strategy therefore needs fair CLV above FanDuel's one-sided margin, about 2% to 12% in the snapshot, over several hundred bets. Prop CLV is weak evidence when no sharp book lists the market ([Unabated](https://unabated.com/content/videos/you-re-using-closing-line-value-wrong), asserted). The Kalshi, Polymarket and Novig columns showed no double-double line for these players that morning ([BettingPros grid](https://www.bettingpros.com/nba/odds/player-props/double-double/)), and no source confirmed whether Pinnacle posts NBA double-doubles, though one data vendor lists it ([SportsGameOdds](https://sportsgameodds.com/blog/nba-betting-markets-api), self-reported). Where no two-way double-double quote exists, a fair price can be derived from two-way points and rebounds markets at another book through the joint model, and CLV on those component markets, which see two-way action, is the more defensible check (inference from [Unabated](https://unabated.com/content/videos/you-re-using-closing-line-value-wrong)).

### Stake caps hold a real edge to about $190 to $2,100 a season

FanDuel's house rules let it scale back any customer's stakes at any time ([FanDuel House Rules](https://massgaming.com/wp-content/uploads/FanDuel-House-Rules-8.24.23.pdf)). Massachusetts regulator data show 0.64% of accounts limited as of December 2024, 57.6% of those held to 1% to 24% of the default stake, and bettors who consistently beat the closing line more likely to have their stake factor cut ([Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/massachusetts-limiting-sports-betting-prediction-markets-exchanges-sharps-box-september-2025), measured). At a 2024 Massachusetts roundtable, operators named a better model or more information among their reasons for limiting, and FanDuel said it limits a smaller share than BetMGM's roughly 1% ([ESPN](https://www.espn.com/sports-betting/story/_/id/41231266/espn-sports-betting-news-sportsbooks-defend-practice-limiting-sharp-customers), self-reported by operators). Professional bettor Gadoon Kyrollos posted that FanDuel's Cory Fox acknowledged limiting customers with a better model ([X](https://x.com/spanky/status/1833889459969970514), secondhand). Prop limits typically sit below spread limits on the same game ([ESPN 2025](https://www.espn.com/nba/story/_/id/46785871/nba-books-review-most-vulnerable-bets-wager-limits)). DraftKings says customer-specific limits touch under 0.15% of accounts, while a professional reported a $25 cap there ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/online-betting-apps-losers-perks-limiting-winners/)), and FanDuel reports a $5 median bet ([Casino.org](https://casino.org/news/fanduel-push-backs-on-high-roller-image-median-bet-is-just-5), self-reported by FanDuel). In the one academic test with real money, soccer books capped the authors at $1.25 to $11.11 within months ([Kaunitz, Zhong and Kreiner](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.02824), preprint, measured). Proving an edge through CLV is the same behavior that triggers the cut. At -250 to -700, a real 2 to 3 point edge over 330 to 495 bets is worth about **$190 to $2,100 a season at $25 to $100 maximum stakes** (computed), comparable to a few months of an NBA prop simulator reported at about $249 a month ([BetSmart review](https://www.betsmart.co/tool-reviews/unabated)).

### Line shopping, news timing and boosts are the routes left

What remains are routes that change the bot's design rather than its model. Line shopping comes first. FanDuel operates district-wide in DC except on federal land such as the National Mall ([BetVirginia](https://www.betvirginia.com/news/betmgm-caesars-launch-apps-in-dc-market-joining-fanduel)), and it has been one of seven district-wide books since bet365 launched in September 2026 ([SBR](https://www.sportsbookreview.com/news/bet365-launches-sportsbook-washington-dc-sept-8-2026/)). In the project's October 29 snapshot, FanDuel's Yes was shorter than DraftKings' on 17 of 27 shared players, longer on 9 and equal on 1, and 10 players differed by 2 or more points of implied probability, led by Sabonis at 87.5% against 81.4% and Cameron Boozer at 38.2% against 34.0% (project file `data/odds/dd_prices.csv`, computed). Against the same theScore Bet fair range, DraftKings' -438 on Sabonis was worth -1.6% to +1.7% while FanDuel's -700 was worth -5.4% to -8.5% (computed), so the choice of book moved expected value more than the choice of de-vig method did. News timing is second. Opening lines misprice absences ([Dare, Dennis and Paul](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v13y2015icp130-136.html)), and Sabonis's Yes moved from an opening -354 to a -560 consensus by mid-morning, about 7 points of probability ([BettingPros Sabonis](https://www.bettingpros.com/nba/props/domantas-sabonis/double-double/), measured). No source measures how fast books reprice double-doubles, though, and market makers open with low limits ([SoBrief summary of The Logic of Sports Betting](https://sobrief.com/books/the-logic-of-sports-betting), asserted). Boosts are third. FanDuel has run 30% and 50% profit boosts on 2+ leg double-double parlays ([FanDuel Research](https://fanduel.com/research/fanduel-nba-promo-offer-two-50-profit-boosts-for-nba-games-on-10-30-24), excerpt). A three-leg parlay of -700 legs moves from -8.3% to about +0.7% with a 30% boost and +6.8% with a 50% boost if each leg is truly 85%, but sits at -17.7% unboosted if each leg is 82% (computed). Unboosted, parlays compound the margin: three legs at -5% each make -14.3% (computed).

On the evidence available, a bot that ranks players by double-double probability and bets the top two or three at FanDuel starts with negative expected value. The research found no study, backtest or verified record of double-double Yes betting or a nightly top-N strategy, and the observed prices, realized rates and margin data all point against the near-lock tier. A different bot is defensible: one that prices every listed player, bets only where calibrated probability beats the top price across DC books by more than the margin under both de-vig methods, logs FanDuel's Yes, the top available price and a two-way fair close on every pick, and stakes small. Its first season produces information, not income.

## Conclusion

The evidence moves the project's center of gravity. Predicting who records a double-double is the part the market already does well, so the bot's real task is narrower: find the nights when a calibrated probability and the top available price disagree by more than a margin FanDuel does not display. That shifts effort from model sophistication toward three systems that are less interesting to build and more decisive: a minutes distribution that reacts to the injury report and the spread, a two-way price log that makes fair CLV computable, and a selection rule keyed to edge rather than likelihood. The gate your strikeout review set (positive CLV over 100 tracked picks at real prices before scaling stakes) needs one amendment for this market. The CLV has to clear the one-sided margin, measured against a two-way fair price under two de-vig methods, because Whelan's data show that modest positive CLV can still lose.

The first season has a second payoff that is easy to miss. The research found no public dataset that pairs FanDuel double-double prices with outcomes and two-way closes, so a pre-registered nightly log of FanDuel's Yes, the top DC price, a two-way fair close and the result would answer the question this report cannot: whether the shading seen on October 28 and 29 is FanDuel's standing practice or two nights of noise. If the shading persists, the profitable action on many nights is to bet elsewhere or pass, and the bot's measurable value includes the losses it declines to take. That mirrors the strikeout season, whose result came from the cushion in the floor strategy rather than from out-forecasting the line.
